** The World Economic Forum (WEF)
An international, non-profit organization founded in 1971 to improve the state of the world through public-private cooperation. It acts as a platform for political, business, academic, and civil society leaders to collaborate on global, regional, and industry agendas, focusing on areas like growth, resilience, and technology. The Forum is renowned for its annual meeting in Davos, which brings together thousands of leaders to discuss and solve pressing global issues, though it has faced criticism for focusing on the interests of a corporate, transnational elite.

Key purposes and functions of the WEF include:
    * Public-Private Cooperation: Bridging the gap between government, business, and civil society to 
    address complex global challenges that require collaborative action.
    
    * Setting Global Agendas: Shaping economic, political, and social agendas, most notably through its 
    annual meeting in Davos, Switzerland.
    
    * Addressing Global Challenges: Driving initiatives on key issues, including economic growth, climate 
    change, technology, and health.
    
    * Convening Leaders: Providing a neutral, independent platform for networking and building trust among 
    global leaders, often termed a hub for the "global elite" to discuss economic and social issues.
    
    * Stakeholder Theory Promotion: Advocating for business practices that consider the interests of all 
    stakeholders—employees, suppliers, and the community—not just shareholders. 


In 2017, the WEF in Davos attracted considerable attention when, for the first time, a head of state from the People's Republic of CHINA was present at the alpine resort.
