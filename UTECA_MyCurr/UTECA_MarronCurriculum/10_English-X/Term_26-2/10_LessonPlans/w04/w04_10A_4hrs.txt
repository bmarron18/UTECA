	"The player is lost; the watcher is lucid."
	当局者迷，旁观者清。(Dāngjúzhě mí, pángguānzhě qīng / 'player confused; spectator clear')





From the "Biography of Yuan Xingchong" in the Old Book of Tang (《旧唐书·元行冲传》), this points out that a person involved in a matter usually does not have a comprehensive overview of it, due to too much concentration on the details, while the onlookers, who have a calmer and more objective attitude, have a better grasp of what is going on.

** Basic Prompt Engineering
  You could think about developer and user messages like a function and its arguments in a programming 
  language.
    	-developer messages provide the system's rules and business logic, like a function definition.
    	-user messages provide inputs and configuration to which the developer message instructions are 
	applied, like arguments to a function.
	-an "prompt" to AI contains BOTH message types

  Developer messages (AI role play instructions; system instructions)
	-gives the model high-level instructions on how it should behave while generating a 
	response, including tone, 
	- goals, and examples of correct responses. Any instructions provided this way will take 
	priority over a prompt in the input parameter.
		
  User messages (task instructions; input parameters)
		Ant text, image, or file inputs to the model that are used to generate a 
		specific response.

She is taken care of by her nanny, Amah, dresses in fine clothes, and is surrounded by a wealthy family that can afford to perform all of the important societal rituals. She even has younger step-sisters from her father's concubines. Ying-Ying and her family will participate in the (harvest) Moon Festival; an extravagant affair that includes an outing on a boat that "looked like a floating teahouse" and an evening of theater starring the Moon Lady. Ying-Ying is especially primped and groomed for the occasion by Amah who also gives her strict rules of behavior and the promise of a secret wish fulfilled awarded by the Moon Lady herself. --- Rules of the Game: Waverly Jong (Ch 5)
Waverly and her family live in Chinatown. The family has modest means but is well-fed and comfortable. As a result of a nearly-discarded chess set, a Christmas gift from the local church Santa, Waverly begins to learn chess. At first her brothers must explain the moves and gladly accept Lifesavers for Waverly's losses. But one day Waverly meets Lau Po who shares his great chess secrets with her. She is intoxicated. Waverly rapidly advances to tournament play and becomes a national champion at nine years-old! The community, and especially her mom, are very proud of her. Her family makes many concessions so that Waverly can continue to study and play chess. Waverly seems to accept these concessions as matter-of-fact events perhaps not recognizing the sacrifices being made on her behalf. As Waverly's fame spreads, Waverly begins to feel that she is constantly being put up for exhibition by her mom. One day things come to a head. Waverly harshly states that if her mom wants to show off, she should learn to play chess herself. Waverly, filled with rage and shame, runs off for a few hours. When she returns, she finds that her mom simply ignores her, in essence revoking her special privileges. Initially hurt, Waverly recovers and plots her next move.
