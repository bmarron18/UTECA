	"As distance tests a horse's strength, time reveals a person's character."
		
	路遥知马力，日久见人心。(Lùyáo zhī mǎlì, rìjiǔ jiàn rénxīn)
	['road distant know horse strength, days old see man heart']









From the anonymous work "Fight Gratitude" (《争报恩》) of the Yuan Dynasty (1279–1368), when Mongol horsemen ruled China, this saying tells us that a long period of testing is needed to understand someone's nature and capabilities.

--- Rules of the Game: Waverly Jong (Ch 5)
Waverly and her family live in Chinatown. The family has modest means but is well-fed and comfortable. As a result of a nearly-discarded chess set, a Christmas gift from the local church Santa, Waverly begins to learn chess. At first her brothers must explain the moves and gladly accept Lifesavers for Waverly's losses. But one day Waverly meets Lau Po who shares his great chess secrets with her. She is intoxicated. Waverly rapidly advances to tournament play and becomes a national champion at nine years-old! The community, and especially her mom, are very proud of her. Her family makes many concessions so that Waverly can continue to study and play chess. Waverly seems to accept these concessions as matter-of-fact events perhaps not recognizing the sacrifices being made on her behalf. As Waverly's fame spreads, Waverly begins to feel that she is constantly being put up for exhibition by her mom. One day things come to a head. Waverly harshly states that if her mom wants to show off, she should learn to play chess herself. Waverly, filled with rage and shame, runs off for a few hours. When she returns, she finds that her mom simply ignores her, in essence revoking her special privileges. Initially hurt, Waverly recovers and plots her next move.

--- The Voice from the Wall: Lena St. Clair (Ch 6)
Growing up in Oakland, Lena seems to have a morbid fascnation with death after she learns her grandfather had a beggar killed. Lena's mom seems to add to this state of the macabre by instilling any number of fears in Lena. Eventually Lena begins to see with "her Chinese eys" and what she sees are scary portraits of everyday life. She becomes rather withdrawn but not as much her mom who, in Lena's eyes, has become a ghost. Lena's American dad is oblivious to these happenings and is excited to move the family to a new apatment in a wealthier part of town. The family is moving up in the world! In the new apartment Lena's mom become increasingly dissatified with the aprtment and its the furnishings and begins an endless phase of reorganizing. The new apartment is rather noisy both from street noises and from the neighbors, and Lena, who once had a comforting relation with the regularties of the sounds from the street, is moved to a new area where she hears the constant and ferocious fighting of the neighbors. Lena believes the neighbors are on the verge of killing each other each and every night. Meanwhile, Lena learns that her mom who was pregnant has lost the baby. Lena's mom is plunged into despair and loses her vitality day by day until she no longer gets out of bed and must be hospitalized. For Lena, her mom truly has become ghost. One night a loud knock at the door reveals the neighbor Teresa, a girl slightly older than Lena who explains her mom has kicked her out of the house. Lena is horrified but Teresa seems unconcerned and uses Lena's bedroom window to access the fire escape. Teresa explins that her plan is to disappear for a few hours and then sneak back into her room via the fire escape. Later that evening when Lena hears Teresa and her mom engaged in a confrontation of tears and hugs, Lena realizes that these people actually love each other and this gives Lena the courage to begin pulling her mom back to life.
