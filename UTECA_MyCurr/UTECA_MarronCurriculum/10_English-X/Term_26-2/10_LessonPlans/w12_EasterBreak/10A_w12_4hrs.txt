** Chinese Identity [for final] --------------------------------------------------
	* Read The Construction of Chinese and Non-Chinese Identities (p 2-5) in 
	"Wu_Construction-Chinese-NonChinese-1991.pdf" [up to CONSTRUCTION OF CHINESE IDENTITY OVERSEAS]
	* Write one (1) question based on the reading
	* Students in two teams
	* Each group up to the front to answer questions from the other group

Notes
	- The Chinese concept of "Chinese" is complex 
	- The modern cultural concept of "Chinese," for example, must be understood in the context of 	
	China's recent political history
		1) China needed a modern identity to cope with conditions created by China's
	confrontation with the Western world where concepts of state, citizen are foreign
		2) the identity needs to acknowledge the traditional view of being at the center of
	existence. Chinese have viewed themselves as being at the center, surrounded by
	culturally inferior barbarians at the periphery.
	- Chinese have had an anthropocentric view is based on a deep-rooted sense of belonging to
	a unified civilization that boasts several thousands of years of uninterrupted history
	- Two pieces to Chinese identity 
		1) they feel a connectedness with the fate of China as a nation.
		2) they feel a sense of fulfillment, of being the bearers of a cultural heritage handed down 
		from their ancestors
	- Chinese are the Han, who ultimately absorbed the "barbarians". Because the Han had absorbed people of all languages, customs, and racial and ethnic origins; the meanings of being Chinese in the sense of ethnicity, culture, citizenship, or residence were almost never addressed. Al are Han.
	
	- By the early twentieth century this concept of a "Chinese people" included four major non-Chinese races, descendants of what were formerly referred to as barbarians: the Man (Manchus), the
Meng (Mongolians), the Hui (ethnic groups of Islamic faith in northwestern China), and the Zang (Tibetans)

	-the existence of a superior Chinese culture is, at best, a myth. The Chinese people and
Chinese culture have been constantly amalgamating, restructuring, reinventing, and reinterpreting themselves
